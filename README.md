# 🦃 The Golden Acorns of Maple Hollow Farm

A Thanksgiving digital escape room for **3rd grade**. Students help Pip the turkey collect seven Golden Acorns scattered across the farm so they can open the Feast Barn before sunset.

**Play it:** https://apesfrep-dotcom.github.io/thanksgivingmystery/

## What students practice

| Stop | Skill |
|---|---|
| 🚪 The Farm Gate | Rounding 2- and 3-digit numbers to the nearest 10 and 100 |
| 🎃 The Pumpkin Patch | 3-digit addition, with and without regrouping |
| 🌽 The Corn Maze | Forces and motion: pushes, pulls, magnets, friction |
| 📚 The Storybook Shed | Elements of literature: characters, setting, plot |
| 🍎 The Apple Orchard | Central idea and supporting details in nonfiction |
| 🫐 The Cranberry Bog | Sequence text structure and signal words |
| 🥧 The Farmers' Market | 3-digit subtraction, with and without regrouping (including across zeros) |
| 🏠 The Feast Barn | Measuring mass (grams and kilograms), then retelling the adventure in order |

44 questions total. Plan for about **45–60 minutes**.

## How it works

- **An animated story, not a worksheet.** An opening cutscene shows the wind blowing the acorns out of the barn door. Pip walks the farm map between stops, and every location is an animated scene with characters who move and react: a pumpkin-lifting machine with spinning gears, a corn maze that opens up, a glowing storybook, an owl in an apple tree, a beaver in a cranberry bog, and a pie stand at a market. The sky moves toward sunset as students make progress.
- **Real voices for every character.** Every line, question, answer choice and hint has a recorded voice: a narrator, Pip, Scraps the scarecrow, Owlbert, Bella the beaver, Mr. Ortiz and Grandma Hazel. Characters' mouths move while they talk.
- **Hands-on answering.** Math uses a chalkboard with place-value columns, a big keypad, and scratch boxes for regrouping that hold two digits (like 14). Students can tap a top digit to cross it out, just like on paper. Ordering questions use drag-and-drop cards (or tap a card, then tap a spot). Forces questions have a "Watch it" animation of the scenario.
- **Storybook reader.** The fiction story and nonfiction articles open in an illustrated book with page turns. Students can look back at it anytime.

- **Read-aloud on demand.** Every 🔊 button plays a recorded voice. Nothing is read automatically, so readers do the reading and non-readers can tap for support. Sound effects can be turned off with the 🔔 button.
- **Hints, never answers.** A wrong answer gets a hint. Common mistakes get a targeted hint (for example, forgetting to regroup, or "flipping" digits in subtraction). Hints get more specific with each try, but the answer is never shown.
- **Saves progress.** If the tab closes or the Chromebook restarts, the student picks up where they left off on that same device and browser. Use **Start over** at the bottom of the page to reset it for the next student.
- **Certificate at the end.** It shows the student's name, the date, and about how long they took. Have students show it to you or take a screenshot.
- **No logins, no tracking, no outside sites.** Everything (the page and the audio) is served from this one GitHub Pages address, so there's nothing extra for a web filter to block.

Works best in Chrome or Edge. Read-aloud uses the device's built-in voices.
